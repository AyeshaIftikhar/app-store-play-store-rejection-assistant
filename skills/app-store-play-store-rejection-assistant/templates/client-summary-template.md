# Client-Facing Explanation Template

Use this template to explain a rejection to non-technical stakeholders (product managers, executives, clients). Avoid jargon and focus on impact, timeline, and what is needed.

```
# App Store Rejection Summary

## What Happened

{One-paragraph plain-language explanation of the rejection. Describe what the
reviewer said without technical jargon.}

Example:
"Apple's review team reviewed our app and requested additional information
about how we handle user privacy before they can complete the review. This is
a standard part of the review process and does not mean the app was rejected."

## Impact

- Timeline impact: {Estimated delay in days}
- Current status: {Binary approved / Binary rejected / Review paused /
  App suspended / Appeal submitted}
- Users affected: {None / New users cannot download / Existing users unaffected}

## What We Need to Do

{Bullet-point list of required actions in plain language}

Example:
- Update our privacy policy to describe what data we collect
- Add a screen in the app explaining our data practices
- Submit a response to the review team

## Timeline

{Estimated completion timeline}

Example:
- Fix implementation: 2 days
- Review and testing: 1 day
- Resubmission: 1 day
- Estimated re-review time: 1-7 days (Apple) / 1-3 days (Google Play)

## Next Steps

1. {Action item with owner}
2. {Action item with owner}
3. {Action item with owner}

## FAQ

### Will the app be permanently removed?
{Answer based on severity — for most rejections, no.}

### Will existing users lose access?
{For most rejections, existing users retain access. Only suspensions/removals
affect new downloads.}

### Will this affect our launch date?
{Be realistic about the delay — build in buffer for re-review time.}

### Do we need legal review?
{Yes, if the rejection involves privacy, health, financial data, or children.}
```

## Tone Guidelines

- Be transparent but not alarming — most rejections are routine
- Use "we" language — the team owns the issue together
- Do not blame the reviewer, the store, or any team member
- Provide time estimates with clear caveats (review time is unpredictable)
- If the issue is severe (suspension, account risk), be direct about the seriousness
